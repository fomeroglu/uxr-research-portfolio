# Smart Containers — Field and Generative Research on a Physical Product

**Scope:** Reusable, trackable containers deployed across a national logistics network
**Outcome:** Research shaped the product from concept to fleet — defining what to build, who it was for, and how to improve it once in the field. 3,000+ units carry the research-driven design.

---

## Research question

Before a single unit was built, two questions had to be answered: would organizations actually adopt a reusable container, and would it survive real logistics conditions? Neither could be answered from a desk. The research program addressed both — first through generative and concept work to validate the product direction, then through field research to improve it once it was deployed.

---

## Research arc

**Generative discovery**
Jobs-to-be-done sessions with operators and decision-makers to understand the underlying job before defining the product. Current workflow, decision factors, pains with existing containers, and what each stakeholder was actually trying to accomplish. A competitive benchmark of the reusable container market ran in parallel — surfacing where durability, tracking, and load factor were underserved.

**Viability research**
Competitor pricing, cycle-life data, and internal operational spend gathered to build the economic case. Research provided the competitive and operational evidence; the business team built the cost-per-trip model on top of it.

**Concept testing — two rounds**
Four concepts (metal build, tall form, top-section access, built-in tracking) were tested with operators and decision-makers separately. Operators ranked concepts against real competitor products. Decision-makers were walked through the economics and tracking value alongside the product concept.

A finding emerged from the decision-maker sessions that we didn't set out to test: decision-makers raised asset retrieval on their own — "this is a valuable asset we send out, how do we get it back?" That single finding reshaped not the product, but its target: a non-foldable, trackable asset is right for closed-loop operations where it never sits idle.

**Research to design**
Validated concepts became the physical design brief handed to engineering:
- Metal concept → durable metal build
- Tall concept → tall, stable form
- Top-access concept → top-section access for faster cross-dock
- Tracking concept → built-in tracker for location and asset recovery

**Field pilot and field research**
A small first batch deployed with early adopters in live closed-loop operations — designed to learn before scaling. Four feedback channels ran in parallel: field observation, operator and site interviews, support-call analysis, and telemetry data cross-checked against field findings.

Field research traced failures to handling, not material: to fill trailers, operators pushed containers together hard — a systematic, incentivized behavior the original design had not anticipated. The damage clustered exactly where the observed handling predicted.

**Design improvements**
Each field finding drove a specific design change:
- Nets bulged and snagged in handling → net redesign
- Damage clustered at specific stress points → structural reinforcement at those points, not the whole shell
- Trackers were vulnerable and slow to install → tracker placement optimized for durability and quick install
- Labels and trackers had no home → label pouches and preinstalled tracker holders

---

## Secondary research thread — tracker and telemetry

The container is also the physical mount for an IoT tracker. A parallel research effort supported the digital layer: a structured field evaluation of three tracking vendors over a multi-week live pilot, and behavioral pattern analysis of raw ping data that produced four movement patterns used to shape tracker configuration and cycle counting at scale.

---

## The scaling arc

A deliberate staged approach: small first batch → field research → redesign → validate with next batch → scale. Each stage gated on real adopter feedback before committing the next batch. 3,000+ units carry the research-driven design.
